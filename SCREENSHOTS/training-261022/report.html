Overnight training, 2026-10-21 to 22

10.1 h unattended (scripts/overnight_hill.py): one brain per human body for duels and three-ways (rung <body>_hill, 112 inputs, the nearest opponent only), trained with the action low-pass (smooth 0.6) and a 3x action-rate penalty for human-like motion. Four runs of 400 iterations (1536 worlds; about 1 h 50 min each, slower than the 65 min estimate). No brain was promoted: the game still ships the old fighters.

Summary

Toddler
The new brains move much more smoothly (40-70 % less twitching) and Kim got better at three-way fights, but every new brain loses one-on-one fights against its own old self, so none replaced the old brains.
Child
Kim's run won 47 % of three-ways instead of 41 % and halved its jitter; Trump's and Grandpa's three-way scores did not improve. In duels against the old fighter of the same body every new brain won only 7-26 % of the decided duels (the mirror baseline is about 50 %).
Adult
The duel bar compares against a same-body mirror, but the training duels were only against the other two bodies (duel_share benches b_ or c_, both other bodies), and the 40 ms action filter costs reaction time against an unfiltered opponent that has been tuned for exactly this mirror. Round 2 for Kim restarted from the fighter (no best yet) and did worse. Next: add the own body to the opponent pool.

Why the duels failed: a mismatch, measured

Kim's new brain (kim_hill2) was measured against the other two bodies' fighters, which is what it trained against. It beats Kim's old fighter there, as in the three-ways, with about 30 % less jitter. It only loses the same-body mirror duel, which the training never practised: the duel bar measured the wrong thing for this recipe.
