Graphics and audio pass

261019. Nine of the ten GFX & Audio options; option 8, the summit upgrade in Blender, followed once Blender was running: map report. Captures: Unity Game view at 960 x 2658 (the 9:16 column), or rendered straight from the game camera where an effect lasts under half a second (marked "camera render", no HUD). Numbered pins mark what changed. Nothing here touches the physics: every new component is render or audio only, and inert in the statistics gates.

1. Settings split again: Match and Show

The new switches (slow motion, quality, three mix channels) did not fit the Settings tab, which already filled the sheet. The rules stay in Match; how the match looks and sounds moved to Show. Two layout faults of the old tab are fixed on the way: "Opportunist" overflowed its segment, "Camera cut-aways" wrapped onto two lines.

2. Fighters: seat-colour rim, edge danger, impact flash

Every opaque fighter material (the G1 parts and the two scanned humans) is rebuilt at start on FX/Fighter.shader, a URP PBR shader with four match-driven layers: a fresnel rim in the seat colour, wear, wet, and a hit flash. The rim turns a pulsing red once the fighter's capture point is within 12 cm of the rim, and a red point light follows the fighter furthest past it. Hair and other alpha-tested materials keep their own shader.

3. Wear and the wet look

Wear builds up per part from the pushing force it takes (contacts with other fighters, and the ground for anything but the feet; 2500 N s wears a part fully) and is cleared when a match starts. Scratches are object-space lines that fade once thinner than a pixel. Wet (darker, glossy) comes from being below the sea and dries over 20 s; a fighter out of the sea also drips for a few seconds.

4. Shockwave and lightning

A hard hit (impact strength 0.7 and up) sends a screen-space ring out from it through the ShockwaveFeature renderer feature (added to both URP renderers; the pass is only queued while a wave runs, so it costs nothing otherwise) plus a dust ring along the ground. Once the storm is past half way, or the live weather has a thunderstorm, lightning strikes the sea every 5-12 s: a bolt about 2 px wide whatever the distance, a double flash of a directional light, and thunder from the strike's direction, delayed by the distance.

6. Slow motion, quality and telemetry

TimeWarp slows time to 0.3x for 0.7 s the first time in a round a fighter's capture point crosses the rim heading out, and nearly freezes for 70 ms on the hardest hits (5 rounds: 9 slow-motions, 5 hit stops). Only Time.timeScale changes, so each 2 ms physics step is the same; it never runs in the gates, in replays, or while something else owns the time scale. QualityTier presets set post-processing, shadows, anti-aliasing, particle and decal budgets and (in a player only) the render scale; Auto steps down in a player build when a fight stays under 45 FPS. Each round's telemetry line in the JSONL now carries perf: FPS, mean and p95 frame time, managed KB per frame, tier.

Player performance

Release player (ParityBatch.BuildPlayer, 540 x 960, i7-10750H, -kothPerf 30, six-way scene, quality Auto = High, all effects on): run 1 54.5 FPS mean, frame p50 16.7 ms / p99 27.0 ms, one 2.1 s hitch at launch; run 2 59.1 FPS, p50 16.7 / p99 26.4 ms, max 81 ms. Physics is the heavy part: a 2 ms step of six bodies takes 1.0 ms (p99 2.9), so 477 of the 500 steps/s keep up. The new materials and shaders compile into the player (no missing-shader fallback). There is no earlier six-way measurement to compare against: the last -kothPerf numbers (60 FPS) were for the two-fighter scene.

7. Self-collision audit

Self-collision audit (training/scripts/collision_audit.py, and EditMode CollisionTests on the Unity XML): self-collision is enabled only foot/shin against the other leg (32 pairs per body). G1 and Trump are clean in zero, rest and T-pose; Kim's foot boxes overlap 3.5 mm in her rest pose, a pair the simulation collides, so every Kim episode starts with her feet pressed together. Fixing it changes her physics and needs her next retrain.
